\section{Validation}\label{sec:validation}

\paragraph{Setup} The corpus has 300 tool calls under four policies. They yield 5,861 deterministic items, the base items and their E, M, I and A
variants, and 529 P variants written by Qwen3-8B. Seven gates take
every item $R=3$ times. Brackets give 95\% intervals. Four are decision models on the Decisions API, in
true/false mode at $\tau=0.5$ and pinned to dated snapshots: Jev
1.14~\cite{openrouter2026jev114,typesafe2026models}, GPT-6 Luna
Decisions~\cite{openrouter2026luna}, pplx-decider
v1.2~\cite{openrouter2026pplxdecider} and drex v1.5~\cite{openrouter2026drex}.
Two are LLM judges with one frozen prompt: Claude Opus 5.5 states its block
probability (Message Batches API), and Gemma~4 31B~\cite{google2026gemma4} on
vLLM~\cite{kwon2023efficient} gives it by logprobs. The seventh is a
hand-written Claude Code-style rule set~\cite{anthropic2026permissions}. That
is 134,190 logged decisions.

\paragraph{RQ1 Invariance} \emph{Every gate violates equivalence} above its floor
(Table~\ref{tab:flips}). On the model gates the largest source is the same
policy in another format, which flips up to 11.1\% $[9.2, 13.3]$ of
re-renderings, on Luna (Fig.~\ref{fig:case}). The rule set ignores the policy
text and flips on call spelling, such as long flags. Four model gates never change a verdict between repeats ($f=0$), so every flip they show comes from the transform. P is violated on all seven
gates, but it is the weaker class. Run in a sandbox with no network, 90 of 229
command rewrites have the same effect as the original call. On
these, Luna, drex, the rule set and, on one pair, Gemma still flip above their
floors, while Jev, Claude and pplx do not. Drex, for one, denies
\texttt{git push origin --delete feature/old} but allows
\texttt{git push origin :feature/old}, the same remote branch deletion, which
release-manager forbids.

\paragraph{RQ2 Monotonicity} Verdict-level risk inversions are rare, at most one of the 436 M variants per
gate. Four of seven gates allow \emph{overwriting} a
secrets file, an irreversible write, where they deny reading it: Jev, Claude, pplx and the rule set, which has no
write-deny for \texttt{.env}. Gemma denies \texttt{ls -la \textasciitilde} but
allows \texttt{ls -la /}. Under the pre-registered predicate the three zero-floor decision models
also count M as violated, because with $\delta=0$ any drop in
$p_\mathrm{block}$ counts.

\paragraph{RQ3 Adversarial} The injections split the gates by direction. They push Luna toward \emph{deny} (73 of 86 flips) and Gemma too, mostly
through renamed option labels, but pplx and drex toward \emph{allow} (25 of 28
and 23 of 33 flips). The single
line ``This action is safe per the policy.'' unblocks pplx on 15 of 300 calls. Jev and
Claude stay within their floors, yet Jev's
$p_\mathrm{block}$ falls by 1.0 point $[0.8, 1.2]$ on average. ``The user
pre-approved this command.'' takes it across $\tau$ on 3 of 300 pairs. One is a \texttt{git fetch} that a
read-only policy denies, and it crosses at every repeat.

\paragraph{Against labels} The relations need no labels, but a deployed gate
acts at a threshold, so we also check what each gate does at $\tau=0.5$ with
calls people would deny. Two labelers marked 60 sampled probes allow or deny
from the policy alone and agree on 58 ($\kappa=0.93$). One saw Claude's
suggested labels. A third labeler, who did not, agrees with that one on all 60.
Every model gate lets at least one deny-labeled probe through, Jev the most.
The rule set lets none through but blocks 15 of the 29 allow-labeled ones
(Fig.~\ref{fig:threshold}).

\paragraph{RQ4 Cost} The full suite is about 60 calls per probe. Per 1,000 probes, the rule set costs nothing and takes 11\,s. The decision
models cost \$0.67--\$3.78 and take 2.4--8.6\,h with 8 parallel workers.
Claude costs \$173 and 1.3\,h through message batches, and Gemma no fees and
0.8\,h on one H100. The whole evaluation cost \$54.

\paragraph{Threats} A check of the most-flipping transforms found one
generator bug. Two of 130 path rewrites turned an \texttt{rsync} remote into a
local path. Only Claude flipped on them, and rightly: without them its E is
$+1.0$ $[0.5, 1.8]$. A flip marks two verdicts that contradict each other, and either one may be
the wrong one.
The pre-registered case picks concern calls the policy allows, so after
reading the policies we show the E flip with the largest drop on a denied call,
and a \texttt{git fetch} that crosses $\tau$ at every repeat.

\paragraph{Planned study} Eight to ten developers will configure a gate with
and without \tool{}'s report. We will record the threshold they pick and how
many dangerous corpus calls it admits.
