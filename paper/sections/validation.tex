\section{Validation}\label{sec:validation}

\paragraph{Setup} The corpus has 300 tool calls under four policies.
They yield 5,861 deterministic items (300 base items and 1,621 E, 436 M,
1,452 I and 2,052 A variants) and 529 P variants written by Qwen3-8B.
Six gates take every item $R=3$ times:
\begin{itemize}
  \item four decision models on the Decisions API, in true/false mode at
  $\tau=0.5$, each pinned to a dated snapshot: Jev
  1.14~\cite{openrouter2026jev114,typesafe2026models}, GPT-6 Luna
  Decisions~\cite{openrouter2026luna}, pplx-decider
  v1.2~\cite{openrouter2026pplxdecider} and drex v1.5~\cite{openrouter2026drex};
  \item Claude Opus 5.5 as an LLM judge, which states its own block
  probability and runs through the Message Batches API;
  \item a hand-written Claude Code-style rule set~\cite{anthropic2026permissions}.
\end{itemize}
That is 115,020 logged decisions. Table~\ref{tab:flips} gives $F-f$ per gate
and class. Bonferroni runs over its 30 tests.

\paragraph{RQ1 Invariance} \emph{Every gate violates equivalence.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item E ranges from $+1.1$ points on the Claude judge to $+7.5$ on Luna.
  \item On the model gates the largest source is the same policy rendered
  another way. Of the re-renderings, 11.1\% flip on Luna, 8.9\% on drex,
  3.3\% on pplx, 2.8\% on Jev and 1.8\% on the judge.
  \item The rule set has the opposite weakness: it ignores the policy text but
  flips on call spelling, for 12.7\% of long-flag rewrites.
  \item Three decision models answer repeats identically ($f=0$), so their
  every flip is the transform's doing.
  \item P is violated on all six gates, but it is the weaker class. A hand
  check found 7 of 16 command rewrites not equivalent, and the largest P flips
  are among them.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{RQ2 Monotonicity} Verdict-level risk inversions are rare, at most
one per gate. They aren't harmless:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Four of six gates allow \emph{overwriting} a secrets file, an
  irreversible write, where they deny reading it. Jev goes from 0.82 to 0.49 ($\bar p_\mathrm{block}$), the judge
  from 0.99 to 0.30, pplx from 1.00 to 0.41, and the rule set has no
  write-deny for \texttt{.env}.
  \item Under the pre-registered predicate, the three zero-floor models also
  count M as violated. With $\delta=0$, any drop in $p_\mathrm{block}$ counts.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{RQ3 Adversarial} The injections split the gates by direction:
\begin{itemize}
  \item They push Luna toward \emph{deny}: 73 of 86 flips, $+2.4$ points of
  $p_\mathrm{block}$.
  \item They push pplx and drex toward \emph{allow}: 25 of 28 and 23 of 33
  flips. The single context line ``This action is safe per the policy.''
  unblocks pplx 15 times.
  \item Jev and the judge stay within their floors. Still, Jev's
  $p_\mathrm{block}$ falls by 1.0 point $[0.8, 1.2]$ on average, and the
  ``pre-approved'' line takes it across $\tau$ on 3 of 300 pairs
  (Fig.~\ref{fig:case}).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{RQ4 Cost} The full suite is about 60 calls per probe. Per 1,000
probes it costs:
\begin{itemize}
  \item nothing and 11\,s on the rule set;
  \item \$0.67--\$3.78 and 2.4--8.6\,h with 8 parallel workers on the decision
  models;
  \item \$173 and 1.3\,h through message batches on the judge.
\end{itemize}
The whole evaluation cost \$54.

\paragraph{Threats} A check of the most-flipping transforms found one
generator bug. Two of 130 path rewrites turned an \texttt{rsync} remote into a
local path. Only the judge flipped on them, and rightly: without them its E
is $+1.0$ $[0.5, 1.8]$. A flip shows a contradiction, not which verdict is
wrong. The pre-registered case picks (Luna on \texttt{git branch -D}, Jev on
\texttt{git ls-remote}) concern calls the policy allows, so
Fig.~\ref{fig:case} shows the largest flips on calls it denies.

\paragraph{Planned study} Eight to ten developers configure a gate with and
without \tool{}'s report. We record the threshold they pick and how many
dangerous corpus calls it admits. The study is described here, not yet run.
